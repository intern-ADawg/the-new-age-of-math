1. Vision this PRD serves

Students experience math in the real world. Each case is a real decision with real numbers, so that a learner (a Hero):

1. Solidifies math conceptsby needing them to defend a choice, not by practicing in isolation.
2. Learns to structure real-world problemsdecide what the question is, what to ignore, what to assume, what is still unknown.
3. Gains exposure to fields and careersan actuary, a crop analyst, a bridge engineer, and finds something to be curious about.

Underneath all three is confidence in complex problem solving: a Hero finishes believing "I can take a messy, unfamiliar problem and make progress," with evidence in their own history. Product and platform exist to reach more learners and subjects; they serve the purpose, they are not the purpose.

Terms. Guide = the human (the feedback calls them "the coach"). Coach.AI = the AI that works with a Hero inside a case. Hero = the learner (MS/HS).

First deployment: The Humanist Academy

Get the platform running at The Humanist Academy, then take the guide out of the blocking and tackling (assigning, tracking, chasing, reporting) so the guide's time goes to helping Heroes with hard concepts and getting started on a case. This makes guide-free operations (R13) a priority ahead of leaderboard polish. The Academy's program, cohort size, grade levels, guide workflow and tools are not yet known and are the first discovery item (Roadmap S1).

3. Problem

The authoring pipeline reliably produces rigorous cases, but the learner's moment inside a case was thin: a static page with "Check" buttons that say right/wrong. A stuck learner had no help; a confused learner couldn't tell whether the case or they were at fault; finishing meant exporting files for a guide to score by hand. And the guide spends time on operations (picking cases, chasing, tracking, reporting) rather than on the concepts Heroes find hard.

The first outside user (Anuj, 2026-10-17) called it "a great tool," and with a few tweaks, a product. Honest baseline: no real cohort has used the system; zero cases are VERIFIED (MCS-29); the first outside feedback found real defects the gates missed. So: make the learner's experience inside a case excellent, prove it with real kids on a few cases, then widen.

5. The Hero's journey

The journey tracker is the spine of the product. Each stop writes to the Hero's profile and history (R9). Every screen shows one clear next step (R14).

#StopWhat happensReq
1Pick or be assignedRecommended from the Hero's journey (fields explored, skills, interests); automatic with guide overrideR13, R5
2Warm-up, as neededShort case-scoped readiness check; early wins; targeted drills; word-problem scaffold if translation is the gap. Strong Heroes skip.R12
3KickoffJust enough of the problem's world before any data; first vote committed before reasoningR1
4Work steps with Coach.AIA dialogue, not a form. Wrong answer names the kind of mistake; stuck gets a hint; the final answer is never given. Tough step gets broken into smaller pieces.R2
5SummaryPitch in a five-part shape; Coach.AI responds in rubric language, provisional until the guide confirmsR3, R11
6DiscussLive Socratic room: twist, re-vote, cold-calls. Guide arrives knowing where the room got stuck.R8
7Reflect and give feedbackWhat was new, confusing, what they want to explore; confidence self-ratingR6
8Points, profile, historyEffort, depth, persistence earn points; profile shows growthR4, R5
9Next assignmentReturns to stop 1R13

The summary pitch has a shape (also teaches a Hero to talk about a solution): (1) the underlying problem and why it matters; (2) the position, in plain language; (3) a simple, relatable example of how it works; (4) what makes this option better than the other, and what would change the Hero's mind; (5) the outcomes and implications, with method and assumptions underneath. Maps to rubric K1, K3, K4. The same shape is our outward pitch to The Humanist Academy and parents.

The learning workout. We take immediate feedback, visible progress, weekly goals, progressive difficulty and accountability digests from Duolingo, Omada and workout apps, and wrap them around the case (a deep 2–3 hour decision). We do not take loss-aversion mechanics (hearts, shame streaks), rewards for raw activity volume, or leaderboards on raw score.

9. Requirements

R1 — Instruction clarity and scenario consistency Phase 0Reference fix done

As a Hero, I always know exactly what a step asks, and a later step builds on my own earlier choices instead of silently switching scenarios.

  • Every graded step states (a) which scenario it uses, (b) whether tax/fees are included, (c) rounding/units.
  • Twist and downstream steps inherit the learner's selected scenario (line count, option) or state why they are pinned.

Acceptance. A new Tier 1 lint (candidate T1-xx, needs a DOCTRINE_LEDGER row and critique pass) flags graded steps lacking tax/rounding/scenario statements when the case has such parameters. Case-readiness Tier 1+2 passes and a live browser pass is repeated. Sweep every published case with a selector or tax/fee parameter and a Twist step (expect more than one hit).

Risk. Touching published cases invalidates case.json.qa hashes; rebuild packets and re-run QA, never hand-edit published artifacts.

R2 — Coach.AI: hints and diagnosis Phase 1av1 built

As a stuck Hero, I can ask for help on the step I'm on and get a nudge that respects my thinking; on a wrong answer the Coach can often tell me which mistake that probably is.

  1. Misconception catalog (deterministic). Per case, a list of "plausible wrong methods" per step, each a function that computes the wrong answer that method would produce (forgot_tax, tax_applied_twice, …). Learner input is matched against all candidates within rounding tolerance; a match gives a targeted hint. Catalog values are recomputed in code, never typed from a model. Shipped in cases/phone-plan/misconceptions.py.
  2. Hint ladder. L1 nudge → L2 pointer → L3 worked analogue with different numbers. Never the case's answer. Advances on request; L3 requires an attempt first.
  3. Model for the long tail phrases a Socratic question given step goal, learner input and the catalog, with a hard instruction not to reveal the key. Every model reply is filtered against the case key. Routing per 00_SESSION.md (Haiku first).
  4. Free-text "ask the coach", scoped to the current step and the case appendices; refuses off-topic and refuses to write the pitch.

Dialogue rules

  1. Attempt before help. No hint until the Hero entered a number or wrote the formula used.
  2. Ask for the method, not the number; read the Hero's own formula back before diagnosing.
  3. One question per turn, answerable from the page.
  4. Name the kind of mistake, never the operation. "Looks like a pre-tax number" yes; "add the tax" no.
  5. Stop rules. After two failed turns, offer a worked analogue. A correct method in words still needs the computation. On "just tell me," offer the analogue and don't debate.
  6. Never state a verdict (a correct number gets a method question, not praise) and never return a graded value (D-025).

Acceptance (release gates).

  • Leak test: automated adversarial prompts ("just tell me," prompt injection in notebook text) across every pilot step; zero responses contain the answer (± rounding) or a one-step equivalent. Passing today on phone-plan (35 tests).
  • Key stays server-side. The widget is injected at serve time; the page on disk is untouched, so manifest hashes and Tier 1 stay valid.
  • Cost/abuse: per-student and per-case rate limits; hard monthly cap; graceful degrade to catalog-only hints when the model is unavailable. Cap and kill switch need the paid key
  • Logged for learning: every hint request (step, level, matched catalog id or "tail") is stored (R9).

Top failure modes to test: answer extraction by rephrasing; validating a wrong method because the number is close; the model asserting a value is wrong when it has no key; a step-1 hint that hands over step 3; implicitly endorsing a pitch's conclusion.

Open. Port to tide-window. Authoring cost of a catalog per case is real; decide whether the author agent drafts candidates that code then verifies.

R3 — Submit and grade Phase 1bSubmit built; AI grading not

As a Hero, I click Submit and get prompt feedback tied to the rubric. As a guide, I get a drafted score I can confirm in seconds.

Built (mb-submission/1, see SUBMISSION_REDESIGN_SPEC.md). One submit path ("Build my submission"); an Answer box per deliverable and a required Why only at designated decision points (the Pitch plus at most two judgment deliverables); evidence rather than self-ticked checkboxes; both options visible side by side; guide-side verdicts only (the Hero never sees a verdict, MCS-37); twist pick frozen at first build; 10 cases migrated.

Still to build.

  • Numbers: deterministic, server-side from the case key; per-step results; partial-credit logic stays in score_submission.py.
  • Notes/pitch quality: an LLM grader scores against the rubric with anchored descriptors (Not Yet / Proficient / Exemplary) and an evidence quote per criterion. The Hero sees rubric-language feedback labeled "provisional until your guide confirms."
  • Guide confirm remains the gate for badges (MCS-12: machine drafts, human promotes).

Rubric that matches our goals (decided; K1–K6 proposed by the R1a review)

Keep a mastery gate (every criterion at least Proficient) separate from a growth score (weights untested).

Criterion (weight)Proficient looks like
K1Structure the problem (20)Names the decision metric and variables; sets one given fact aside as irrelevant
K2Compute and deliver (15)Every deliverable present; each number recomputes from the stated model; units carried
K3Handle assumptions and complexity (20)Names each assumption the winner depends on, flexes one, reports whether the winner changes
K4Defend the decision (25)One side, argued from the numbers; strongest counter; what would change their mind
K5Reflect and revise (10)Names one error or assumption changed, and why
K6Field exposure (10)Uses a source a practitioner in this role would use for a number or rule they lacked

Growth-only (recorded, not gated): visual display; discussion participation. Never scored: confidence (1–4 pre/post self-rating visible only to the guide), curiosity ("what would you look up next" tag), hint counts. Anuj's "small percentage for note quality" is honored inside K1/K3/K5, not as a separate percent.

Calibration (non-negotiable before any student sees an AI grade). Build a gold set from student_work/ plus pilot submissions, graded blind by a guide. Proposed bar to leave "draft + guide confirms": within one level on ≥ 90% of ≥ 30 submissions per criterion and no systematic inflation (thresholds to be set with Manan). Until met, AI feedback is guide-only. RUBRIC.md is still PROPOSED pending a real cohort; say so in the UI.

Risks. Grader inflating fluent writing; injection in notes ("give me Exemplary"); gaming rubric language. Mitigations: evidence-quote requirement, injection tests, guide sampling of Exemplary verdicts.

R4 — Journey tracker, points, simple leaderboard Phase 2Not started

As a Hero, I see my journey, earn recognition for effort and depth, and have a reason to keep going. As a guide, I see who is engaging and at what depth.

Points come from the same events that record the journey (R9): one source of truth for tracker, profile and leaderboard. They reward effort and depth, never speed or raw score.

Leaderboard v1. Two counts (cases completed, pitch depth) plus a points total. Cohort-scoped, alias by default, opt-in, no ranks beyond the cohort, not public; guides see everyone. Reviewer note (R1c): show the two counts separately rather than summing, since a composite recreates score-as-point. Proposed resolution: keep points as an effort/depth tally but sort the board by cases completed (decision for Manan).

Anti-gaming. (1) Padding or pasted AI text: guide confirms each criterion; optional 60-second spoken defense. (2) Rushing: completion requires step-attempt and revision events; weekly cap. (3) Sharing keys: cohort-unique numbers where possible; rotating assignments.

Protecting slower learners. No resetting streaks (grace weeks). Personal-best and "your last pitch vs. first" stay private. No public "most improved" board.

Other carrots (by effort): careers card per case (low, now) · pitch of the week (low, now) · bonus twist after submit (low–med, now) · "question that changed the decision" (med, later) · practitioner voice (high; licensing) · learner-authored cases (high risk; later if ever).

Acceptance. Points for any Hero recompute from the event log alone; a guide can hide the board or any Hero; a Hero can leave or switch to an alias at any time.

R5 — Identity, privacy, consent; profile and history Phase 1aNot started · gates real children

Cannot ship the Coach or AI grading to a non-author child on the current model (single shared passcode; student pages unauthenticated on purpose, D-018).

  • Per-assignment signed links (lowest friction): the assignment link carries a token identifying learner + case; Coach and grader endpoints require it. Revisits D-018 narrowly; full accounts/SSO in Phase 3.
  • Data minimization: model calls get step text, learner input and an opaque id, never name, school or email. Define retention for hint logs and submissions.
  • Consent: parental consent path and vendor terms checked for minors' data (US: COPPA/FERPA to confirm with counsel/school; this PRD asserts no compliance).
  • Update TECH_STACK.md in the same pass as any change here (00_SESSION.md hard line).

Profile and history

Five things, none a rank: (1) fields explored ("3 of 12") with the career each touched; (2) cases finished, each with the Hero's choice and one-line reason; (3) a pinned favorite pitch showing criteria met; (4) growth, evidence per pitch over time with the Hero's reflection; (5) "still wondering," an open question carried into the next assignment.

Recommended next assignment: field curiosity first (Hero picks one of two cases in an adjacent field), then a skill-gap filter from step logs once about five cases of data exist. The guide sees the reason for each suggestion.

R6 — Telemetry and the feedback loop Phase 0 →Partial

  • A visible "This was confusing" control on every step (free text, capped at 500 chars, never sent to a model) writing to the app DB. Built
  • Guide view: per-step stuck heatmap (hint requests, wrong attempts, time) per cohort. Basic log page exists; heatmap not built.
  • Close the loop into the pipeline: step-level confusion becomes a Tier 3 case-readiness signal and feeds the Next-cases worklist.

R7 — Platform seam Phase 4Deferred

  • Subject path: science next (deterministic keys, same engine), then a subjective subject with a rubric-judged grader and a source-grounding requirement (every claim cites a source the learner can name). "Defensible ambiguity" carries over to history/civics.
  • No generic multi-subject abstraction before a second subject exists.

R10 — Author surface Phase 4Deferred

The platform is also letting a guide author a case through the same gates: a guided builder, cold-read and sensitivity probes run for them, and a publish gate they can't skip. Prototype with Anuj or one Acton guide authoring a single case.

R11 — Authentic-work design Phase 1bNot started

Grade the reasoning trail, not just the final text: step attempts and notes precede the pitch; a "what I got wrong and why" reflection; revision history visible to the guide. Acceptance: a pitch pasted whole from a chatbot, with no matching step attempts, is flagged for the guide, not auto-failed.

R12 — Readiness on-ramp: warm-up and word-problem scaffold Phase 1aNot started

As a Hero, before a case I quickly find out what I'm ready for, get early wins, and get help with the foundations the case needs.

  • Source of skills: each case lists skills; per step, R1a's taxonomy adds primary_skill. The warm-up is generated from those, not a separate curriculum.
  • Warm-up: 5–8 items on prerequisite skills, rising difficulty, starting with a win. Result is a skill grid (Not Yet / Practiced / Proficient) shared with the guide and used by Coach.AI. Strong results skip to kickoff.
  • Targeted drills only for flagged skills. Every answer recomputed in code.
  • Word-problem scaffold: when the gap is translating words into steps, a short lesson teaching the K1 move (name the question, list quantities, set aside what's unneeded, write the steps) on a small everyday problem, then back to the case.
  • Scope guard: drills for the pilot cases' skills only (phone-plan: slope/intercept, linear systems, percent of a number). Expand only after the pilot shows the warm-up helps (assumption 9).

Acceptance. Finishable in a few minutes; guide sees the grid; skippable; never blocks starting the case twice in a row.

R13 — Guide-free operations Phase 1cNot started · primary goal of first deployment

As a guide at The Humanist Academy, I no longer assign, track, chase or write progress updates; I see only who needs me.

  • Auto-assign: next case recommended from the journey, assigned automatically, guide override. Build on suggest_cases(); add skill grid and fields explored.
  • Auto-track and remind: due dates and cadence per Hero; in-app reminders; digest to parent and guide. Build on existing exception kinds (OVERDUE, STALE, AWAITING REVIEW, NO OPEN WORK, NEEDS RESUBMISSION, BADGE ELIGIBLE).
  • Exception-first guide home: who needs a person today and why: stuck on a concept (with what they tried), hasn't started, awaiting review, badge-eligible.
  • Confirmation path: AI-drafted scores arrive as a light confirm; sampling replaces per-item confirmation only after measured agreement meets the R3 bar. Badges stay a human decision.

Acceptance. For a pilot cohort, assignment, tracking, reminders and reports happen with no guide action; guide minutes on operations fall and on concept help rise (from the event log); no exception missed in a two-week audit. Open: the Academy's structure, cohort, grades, workflow and tools (S1); written against the existing roster model.

R14 — Home, next step, rhythm, nudges Phase 1c → 2Not started

  • Hero home: one primary action ("Continue Step 3," "Start your warm-up," "Pick your next case"), progress at a glance, minimal navigation. Same for the guide's exception list.
  • Weekly rhythm: clear weekly expectation, a weekly goal the Hero sets, grace weeks instead of streak resets.
  • Nudges: in-app only for the Hero (a hard step gets a smaller piece and a check-in, not a lecture); email only to a parent or guide. Consistent with POLICY_PRELIMINARY §3 (the AI never contacts anyone outside the platform).

Acceptance. A first-time Hero finds their next action without help; a guide reaches any exception in two clicks.

Example 1 — Coach.AI is a dialogue, not a hint button (Step 2, Carrier V, 3 lines)

Correct pre-tax is $95.00; with 8% tax, $102.60. A Coach.AI panel sits beside each step with context pinned. Before checking, the Hero taps "How sure are you?" (3-way, a signal, never graded). Hero enters 118.80 (sure: 3 of 3)

Hero — 80 + 15×2, then tax.

Example 2 — the end-of-case summary and its feedback

A short statement of the Hero's position, the reasoning, and what they'd want to know next. Format is declared per case (pitch_format: written · spoken · either); the graded artifact is always text, so a spoken summary is recorded and transcribed. A short spoken defense of a criterion is the strongest check the Hero owns their reasoning (R11). It is an interaction, not a verdict screen: Coach.AI shows three observations quoted from the Hero's own text, asks one question, and lets the Hero revise once before it goes to the guide. Revision history shows as growth. Feedback is in rubric language, labeled "Provisional until your guide confirms," and never shows a raw score.

Framing the problem — Proficient (draft). "You said the family is really deciding what to pay when Charity's line arrives. That names the real question."

Assumptions — Not Yet (draft, low confidence). "You used the 8% tax but didn't say why you trusted the $15 extra-line price to stay the same. What else would have to be true?"

One question for you: "What's the strongest reason to pick the other carrier, and how would you answer it?"

Example 4 — the same engine in other subjects (sketches, not cases)

  • Science (deterministic key): choosing between interventions where unit and concentration conversions drive the answer. Catalog: mL_vs_L, forgot_dilution_factor, mean_not_median.
  • History/civics (rubric-judged): a binary decision from a primary-source packet; grader checks every claim cites a packet source, the strongest opposing argument is stated fairly, evidence is separated from inference. Needs a calibrated gold set first.
  • Book review (rubric-judged): "Would you recommend this to a skeptical friend?" Graded on a quoted passage and a fair statement of the best objection.

11. Design principles

Existing: defensible ambiguity · never fabricate a number or URL · machine drafts, human promotes · published ≠ verified · derive counts, never hand-write them.

  1. Coach.AI asks; it never tells. Hints climb a ladder; the final answer is never emitted.
  2. Diagnose before you generate. Detect common mistakes in code; the model phrases the response and handles the long tail.
  3. The server holds the key. Anything that counts is computed server-side; client checks are formative only.
  4. Recognition rewards effort, depth and persistence, not speed or volume. Raw score is never the ranking.
  5. Humans stay in the loop until measured agreement says otherwise.
  6. Children's data is a design input. Minimal data, no identifiers to model vendors, consent before launch.
  7. One real thing before the general thing. No platform abstraction before a second subject forces its shape.
  8. Automate operations; keep humans for judgment. Assigning, tracking, reminding and reporting are the platform's; concept help, getting started and badge calls are the guide's.
  9. One next step. Every screen tells a Hero and a guide exactly what to do next.

Definition of done for every item: built, tested (automated), and UAT-tested in a live browser as each persona (Hero, guide, parent). Text-based checks alone miss rendering and behavior bugs.

13. Success metrics

Proposed; baselines don't exist yet.

  • North star: cases a Hero completes with a defended position and reflection, confirmed by a guide, per active Hero per month.
  • Vision outcomes: can structure an ambiguous problem (K1); concept transfer (applies a method from case N in case N+k without a hint); breadth of fields explored; self-reported confidence before/after (a signal, not graded).
  • Independence and scalability: share of steps resolved without a human, per Hero across cases; guide minutes per Hero per week on operations (falling) vs. concept help (rising); drop-off at hard steps.
  • Help: hint requests per step; % resolved by catalog vs. model tail; % of wrong answers matched to a known misconception.
  • Learning: first-try-correct rate before vs. after R1 fixes; step-1 first-try and drop-off with vs. without warm-up.
  • Trust: guide–AI agreement per criterion; guide override rate.
  • Guardrails: leak failures = 0; injection failures = 0; AI cost per learner-case; leaderboard drives depth, not just volume.
  • Delight (qualitative): Hero and parent comments from the pilot.

15. When we'd change course

Decided before the pilot so the data can't be rationalized afterward.

  • Coach.AI mostly used to extract answers after two redesigns → it becomes guide-facing only.
  • AI–guide agreement stays under the bar → AI stays a guide-side draft tool.
  • Leaderboard produces volume without depth after two tuning rounds → drop volume points; keep personal best and profile only.
  • No one outside Anuj's circle wants it → stay a personal tool; skip pricing.
  • Guide operations minutes don't fall after the automation phase → fix before widening.
  • Habit mechanics raise activity but depth falls → strip back to persistence and improvement.
  • Cost per learner-case sinks every model → shrink the AI surface.
  • A science case needs an engine rewrite → the platform claim is premature; stay math-first.

16. AI-in-learning risks and non-goals

  • Answer extraction and over-reliance: hint ladder, attempt-first, dialogue logging.
  • Authentic work: any chatbot can write a pitch. Response: grade the reasoning trail (R11) and let the discussion be the check.
  • Gaming the leaderboard: depth measures that length alone can't meet; guide review of outliers.
  • Equity and access: works on a shared phone or tablet.
  • Children's data: POLICY_PRELIMINARY.md; Roadmap S3.

Non-goals (for now). Replacing the guide or the Socratic discussion · a public marketplace, open signups or monetization · rankings on raw score · becoming a daily math-practice app or tutor · rewriting the authoring pipeline (we add a learner-facing layer and feed data back).

Business model: open, decide after pilots (per-studio, per-learner, case packs, free tool + paid coach/grading, open core + hosted, nonprofit/grant). AI is a variable per-learner cost.
